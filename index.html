Offenses & Prohibited Acts

Crimes, Acts & Penalties

Understanding gender-based crimes under statutory laws and public space frameworks.

Public Spaces

Catcalling & Public Leering

Unwanted verbal or physical sexual actions performed in streets, public transport, parks, or commercial establishments.

  • Wolf-whistling, catcalling, and intrusive staring.
  • Persistent uninvited requests for contact or personal details.
  • Making derogatory sexual remarks or gestures.
R.A. 11313 (Safe Spaces Act) Fine up to ₱100,000 & community service or arresto mayor.
View Legal Penalties
Workplace & Academic

Quid Pro Quo Sexual Harassment

Demanding sexual favors in exchange for employment, grade advancement, hiring, or favorable treatment.

  • Conditioning hiring or promotion on sexual compliance.
  • Creation of a hostile or intimidating working/learning environment.
  • Abuse of supervisory or academic authority.
R.A. 7877 & R.A. 11313 Imprisonment up to 6 months & administrative dismissal.
View Legal Penalties
Digital & Online

Gender-Based Online Sexual Harassment

Using Information and Communications Technology (ICT) to intimidate, harass, or demean individuals based on gender.

  • Cyberstalking and sending uninvited explicit photos (Cyberflashing).
  • Unauthorized distribution of private intimate photos/videos.
  • Creating fake social accounts to impersonate or defame victims.
R.A. 11313 (Sec. 12) Prision correccional or fines up to ₱500,000.
View Legal Penalties
Institutional Discrimination

Gender Discrimination in Employment

Unfair wage gaps, denial of benefits, or refusal to hire based on gender identity, pregnancy, or marital status.

  • Paying lesser compensation to women for work of equal value.
  • Dismissing female employees due to pregnancy or marriage.
  • Barring women from promotion opportunities based on gender stereotypes.
R.A. 9710 (Magna Carta of Women) Civil damages, administrative sanctions & license revoking.
View Legal Penalties
Public Spaces

Stalking & Unwanted Physical Contact

Physical touch without consent, invading personal space, or persistent unwanted physical tailing.

  • Groping, pinching, or rubbing against another person in public vehicles.
  • Following a person persistently in public or near their residence.
  • Blocking paths or physically restraining movement.
R.A. 11313 (Sec. 11) Arresto mayor in maximum period & fine up to ₱100,000.
View Legal Penalties
Academic Institutions

Academic Harassment & Bias

Gender bias or sexual misconduct perpetrated by faculty, staff, or peer students in schools/universities.

  • Faculty soliciting sexual favors for passing grades or honors.
  • Peer-to-peer sexual harassment on campus or official university events.
  • Exclusion of students from academic opportunities based on gender.
R.A. 11313 Campus Rules Expulsion, revocation of teaching license & criminal filing.

Statutory Frameworks

Key Anti-Harassment Laws

Core legislative acts safeguarding gender rights, dignity, and institutional accountability.

Republic Act 11313
Safe Spaces Act
Enacted 2019

Bawal Bastos Law (Safe Spaces Act)

Expands gender-based sexual harassment protection beyond the traditional workplace to streets, public spaces, commercial establishments, educational institutions, and online cyber platforms.

Key Mandatory Provisions: Establishes Committee on Decorum and Investigation (CODI) in workplaces and schools; mandates installation of reporting mechanisms in LGUs; penalizes street catcalling and cyberstalking.
Republic Act 7877
Anti-Sexual Harassment Act
Enacted 1995

Anti-Sexual Harassment Act of 1995

Declares all forms of sexual harassment in the employment, education or training environment unlawful. Focuses primarily on hierarchical relationships where power dynamics exist.

Key Mandatory Provisions: Declares employers/school heads solidarily liable for damages if they fail to act on reported complaints; penalizes abuse of authority by superiors or faculty.
Republic Act 9710
Magna Carta of Women
Enacted 2009

The Magna Carta of Women

Comprehensive human rights law that seeks to eliminate discrimination against women by recognizing, protecting, and fulfilling their rights, particularly marginalized sectors.

Key Mandatory Provisions: Guarantees equal treatment in employment, prohibits expulsion of pregnant students, mandates non-gender biased educational materials, and sets 5% GAD budget allocation in government.
UN Treaty
CEDAW Convention
Ratified 1981

Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination against Women

Often described as an international bill of rights for women. Defines what constitutes discrimination against women and sets up an agenda for national action to end such discrimination.

Key Mandatory Provisions: Articles 10 & 11 guarantee equal rights in education and employment opportunities, equal remuneration, and protection against maternity discrimination.
